CASE 02 · Acclaro — AI-generated subtitle quality auditingProfessional experience

Classifying defects so feedback becomes actionable

Context: During AI-generated subtitle evaluation, generic free-text feedback made it harder to distinguish defect types and severity. I proposed a structured taxonomy with 18 error categories and severity levels; the framework was adopted by the team.

18error categories proposed, structured and adopted to make subtitle QA findings more specific and consistent.

What the framework supported

The groupings below summarize review dimensions; they are not a verbatim reproduction of the full internal 18-category taxonomy.

01 / ACCURACY

Meaning and completeness

Mistranslation, omission, addition, hallucinated content and altered meaning.

02 / LANGUAGE

Linguistic quality

Grammar, syntax, fluency, unnatural phrasing and register mismatch.

03 / CONTEXT

Context and speaker

Speaker attribution, context mismatch, terminology and inconsistent references.

04 / SUBTITLE

Technical compliance

Timing, segmentation, readability, formatting and subtitle-structure issues.

05 / SEVERITY

Impact assessment

Distinguish critical meaning failures from major defects and minor improvements.

06 / FEEDBACK

Evidence and correction

Record timecode/context, describe the issue and recommend a usable correction.

Example of structured feedback

Anonymized format only; no proprietary subtitle text or client material is reproduced.

FieldExample entry
Issue typeMeaning / omission
SeverityHIGH
EvidenceSource audio and subtitle at the relevant timecode
RationaleRequired semantic information is missing from the output
Recommended actionRestore the missing meaning and re-check the surrounding subtitle context

Quality impact: a shared taxonomy gives reviewers a consistent vocabulary, helps teams distinguish issue types, and turns observations into actionable feedback for quality improvement.

CASE 03 · TeamWorker — Enterprise taxonomy and metadata qualityProfessional experience

Improving findability and content consistency at scale

Context: Enterprise knowledge and customer-service content relied on terminology that needed to reflect how users searched and described products, processes and issues. I expanded and structured the controlled vocabulary using corpus insights and taxonomy principles.

700 → 2,700+controlled-vocabulary terms, built from analysis of more than 2 million monthly customer-service interactions.

Governance approach

  • Analyzed search language and recurring semantic gaps.
  • Expanded the terminology taxonomy and mapped variants to preferred terms.
  • Applied controlled-vocabulary relationships informed by ISO 25964 principles.
  • Supported consistent metadata and terminology use across content workflows.

Reported operational outcomes

  • 67% reduction in zero-result searches.
  • 18% improvement in average resolution time across an operation supporting 30,000+ agents.
  • Prompt and terminology resources supported consistent content and customer-service interactions.

The outcomes are operational results associated with the wider content and knowledge initiative; they should not be interpreted as the isolated effect of a single taxonomy change.

CASE 06 · Brazilian Portuguese — Comparative AI response evaluationIndependent demonstration

Evaluating two fluent answers against explicit criteria

Purpose: This independent demonstration illustrates how I compare AI-generated responses without equating fluency with correctness. The example uses a Brazilian Portuguese grammar prompt and is not presented as paid client work.

Evaluation dimensions

  • Factual and linguistic accuracy.
  • Reasoning and internal consistency.
  • Instruction adherence and completeness.
  • Clarity, relevance and naturalness.
  • Evidence-based rationale for the ranking.

Decision record

  • Identify the exact claim or instruction being assessed.
  • Compare both responses against the same criteria.
  • Record the specific defect or strength.
  • Rank the outputs and explain the decision.

Key principle: pairwise preference should be supported by a traceable quality rationale—not by style preference or superficial fluency alone.
